From Classroom to Field Service: Starting at Advance Business Systems

November 2026 Om Vatish 6 min read

In July 2026 I started as a Field Service & Sales Support / IT Technician at Advance Business Systems in Windsor. After two years of networking labs at St. Clair College, and two years of IT work at the college's Student Representative Council before and during that, I thought I knew what "real IT" would feel like. I was about half right.

The lab never fights back. Production does.

In a lab, the network is exactly as broken as the assignment says it is. In the field, a business network is a decade of decisions made by different people for forgotten reasons. VLANs that don't match the documentation. A firewall policy nobody remembers creating. A server doing three jobs it was never sized for. The skill that transfers from college isn't memorizing commands, it's troubleshooting method: isolate, test one variable, verify, document.

What the work actually looks like

No two days have matched so far. A typical stretch can include:

  • WatchGuard firewalls: policies, site-to-site BOVPN tunnels, VPN troubleshooting
  • Servers and virtualization: Windows Server environments and VMware ESXi hosts
  • Storage: Synology NAS systems, backups, and the occasional failing drive that becomes a data recovery job
  • Workstations: deployments, domain joins, data migrations, and scripting the boring parts with PowerShell
  • Remote support: TeamViewer and SyncroMSP sessions with customers who just want their problem gone
  • The unexpected: wireless bridges between buildings, IP cameras that won't join a meeting, switches with opinions

The SRC years were the real internship

Working IT for the Student Representative Council, first as a technician, then coordinating IT for the Nexus Gaming Arena and campus events, taught me the part college can't: customers. Explaining a problem without jargon. Staying calm when the event starts in twenty minutes and the tech doesn't work. Owning a difficult problem instead of escalating it into the void. Field service is that, with higher stakes and longer drives.

What I'm doing differently now

Documenting everything. In my home lab, I'm the only one who pays for missing notes. At work, the next technician, or future me, inherits every undocumented change. Verifying with the customer, not just the console. A green status light is not the same as a person confirming their systems work. And automating the repeatable: if I've built the same workstation three times by hand, the fourth one is a script.

The goal was never just to fix things. It's to leave every environment a little more reliable, a little more secure, and a little better documented than I found it.

I'll be writing here about the interesting problems as they come up, anonymized, always, because customer networks are their business, not my content. First up: the site-to-site VPN that stopped talking, and how it got fixed.
